
# Combat Progression Playtest

**Development Stage:** Very Early Development  
**Focus:** Combat Progression  
**Stages Tested:** Whispering Woods, Goblin Stronghold

## Playtest 1

## Observations

- Combat felt too easy and boring, providing little incentive to keep watching or engaging with the fights.
- Death had no meaningful consequence because experience was retained and there was no apparent penalty for dying.
- Regular waves were grindable, but their primary purpose appeared to be gaining experience rather than preparing for a meaningful challenge.
- Stage progression required defeating the boss, but the boss encounter lacked the sense of player-controlled preparation I wanted.
- Bosses felt more threatening than regular wave monsters because they had skills, whereas regular waves relied more on enemy numbers.
- I liked having multiple monsters appear simultaneously in some waves.
- HP resets between waves, which limits the potential value of potions during regular wave progression.

## Player Expectations

- Regular waves should provide a useful opportunity to grind and collect resources.
- Players should be able to choose when to challenge a boss.
- Boss encounters should provide a meaningful test of character strength and preparation.
- Death should have consequences without necessarily erasing earned experience.
- Combat difficulty should give players a reason to improve their characters rather than simply watch repetitive fights.


## Playtest 2

### Observations

- The playtest is limited by the availability of only two stages, restricting the variety of progression and gameplay systems that can be evaluated.
- When left running, combat progression still feels similar to the previous playtest, even after combat difficulty was reduced.
- The game can be followed with minimal attention, which is appropriate for an idle RPG. However, the current build offers limited opportunities for meaningful player decisions.
- Future gameplay systems and level-gated unlocks may make progression more engaging by introducing additional ways to develop a character.
- The new Challenge Boss feature provides a more deliberate way to approach stage progression.
- The two available bosses are early-game encounters, so their difficulty is understandable. However, dying to a boss currently has little consequence beyond failing to defeat it.
- Bosses could become more demanding in the mid-to-late game, requiring sufficient survivability and damage output to win within a limited time.
- The current HP visualization communicates health effectively, but its large size and ambiguous color make it visually uncomfortable to look at.

### Player Expectations

- Combat should be able to run automatically without requiring constant attention.
- Player engagement should come primarily from meaningful decisions about character preparation and progression.
- Players should eventually be able to grind specific stages for particular loot or resources.
- Players should be able to choose equipment based on their intended build and the challenges they expect to face.
- Players should be able to select or craft potions that provide useful effects for specific encounters.
- Boss encounters should test whether the player has prepared sufficiently, including both survivability and damage output.
- Boss difficulty should increase as players progress, without making early-game failure unnecessarily punishing.
- HP visualization should remain easy to understand while fitting more naturally into the overall UI.

### Design Insight

The engagement of an idle RPG does not necessarily depend on actively controlling every fight. It can come from deciding how to prepare for future encounters.

Potential sources of meaningful decisions include:

- **Stage selection:** Choosing where to grind based on the loot or resources needed.
- **Potion selection:** Preparing consumables based on the effects needed for a particular encounter.
- **Equipment selection:** Choosing equipment that supports the player's intended build or addresses a boss's challenges.
- **Boss challenges:** Deciding when the character is sufficiently prepared to attempt progression.

The goal is to make combat low-attention while making preparation and progression meaningful.

These systems are design directions for future development and have not yet been fully validated by the current playtest.
